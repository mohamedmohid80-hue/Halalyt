The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, once asked his Companions a question. Is any one of you unable to earn a thousand good deeds every single day?

They asked him, how could any of us earn a thousand good deeds? And he told them. That answer, and five more like it, are what this video is about. Small things you can do every day that are worth thousands of good deeds, all from authentic hadith.

But first, you need to understand how good deeds are counted, because it's very different from how we count anything else.

The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said that when a person intends a good deed and does it, Allah writes it down as ten good deeds, up to seven hundred times, and even more. And if you only intend a good deed but don't manage to do it, Allah still writes it as one full good deed.

So a good deed is never just one. Allah multiplies it. And once you know that, small things start to add up very fast.

Number one. The answer to the Prophet's question.

He said, glorify Allah a hundred times. Say SubhanAllah one hundred times, and a thousand good deeds will be written for you, or a thousand sins will be wiped away.

A hundred SubhanAllah takes about two minutes. You can say it walking to class, sitting in traffic, or waiting for the kettle to boil. Two minutes, a thousand good deeds.

Number two. Reading the Quran, letter by letter.

The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said that whoever reads one letter from the Book of Allah gets a good deed, and every good deed is multiplied by ten. Then he said, I'm not saying Alif Lam Mim is one letter. Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter, and Mim is a letter.

Think about what that means. Surah al-Fatihah alone has more than a hundred letters. So every time you recite it, that's more than a thousand good deeds. And you recite it in every single rakah of every prayer, at least seventeen times a day.

Number three. One sentence, a hundred times.

The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said that whoever says, la ilaha illallah, wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul mulku wa lahul hamd, wa huwa ala kulli shayin qadir, one hundred times in a day, gets the reward of freeing ten slaves, a hundred good deeds are written for him, a hundred sins are wiped away, and he's protected from Shaytan for that whole day until the evening.

And no one comes with anything better than him, except someone who said it even more.

Number four. Praying in congregation.

Praying with the jamaah is twenty seven times better than praying alone. Same prayer, same time, same effort, twenty seven times the reward, just because you prayed it with others. If you live near a masjid, or you're at school or work with other Muslims, pray together whenever you can.

Number five. Words that outweigh hours.

One morning, the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, left his wife Juwayriyah sitting in her place after Fajr, making dhikr. He came back late in the morning and she was still sitting there. He told her that he had said four phrases, three times, that would outweigh everything she had said since the morning.

Those words were, SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi, adada khalqihi, wa rida nafsihi, wa zinata arshihi, wa midada kalimatih. Glory be to Allah and praise be to Him, as many times as the number of His creation, as much as pleases Him, as heavy as His Throne, and as much as the ink of His words.

Say it three times after Fajr. It takes less than a minute.

Number six. The deed that keeps going without you.

The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said that whoever guides someone to something good gets the same reward as the person who does it.

So if you teach your friend one of these dhikr, every time they say it, you get the reward too, without it taking anything away from theirs. And if they teach someone else, it keeps going. One message, one video, one reminder, and you could be earning good deeds from people you'll never even meet.

So let's add up a normal day. A hundred SubhanAllah, a thousand good deeds. Al-Fatihah seventeen times in your prayers, thousands more. A hundred la ilaha illallah. Your prayers in jamaah. Three phrases after Fajr. And every person you've taught any of this to.

None of it costs money. Most of it takes a few minutes. And all of it is multiplied by Allah.

So the question the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, asked his Companions is really a question for us too. Is any one of us unable to earn a thousand good deeds every day?
