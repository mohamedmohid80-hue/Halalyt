This is a statue of Baphomet, made by the Satanic Temple. Look at its hand. Two fingers up. And a lot of people online say this is where the peace sign comes from. So should Muslims stop doing it in pictures? Let's go through the poses everyone does in photos, where each one actually comes from, and which ones you should avoid.

First, the peace sign.

Look closely at the statue's hand again. The two fingers are pressed together, pointing straight up. That isn't the peace sign. It's an old blessing gesture you'll find in church paintings, and the statue copies a drawing made by a French occultist in eighteen fifty-six, where one hand points up and the other points down.

The peace sign is different. The two fingers are spread apart in a V, with your palm facing out. It started in nineteen forty-one, when a Belgian politician asked people on BBC radio to use the letter V as a sign of victory against the Nazis. Churchill made it famous, and years later it became the peace sign.

And Islamic rulings online say the same thing. One fatwa is literally titled, the V sign has nothing to do with Satan.

There's also one catch most people don't know. If you do the V with your palm facing yourself, in the UK, Australia and New Zealand, that's a rude insult, like the middle finger.

So no, the peace sign isn't a devil sign. But the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said, leave what makes you doubt for what doesn't make you doubt. And when a sign looks this close to something on a Satanic statue, and can be an insult depending on how you hold it, the safest thing is to just avoid it.

Second, the devil horns.

This one is the index finger and the pinky up, with your thumb holding the other two fingers down. It comes from Italy, where it was used as a gesture to protect against the evil eye, or to put the evil eye on someone. Then in the nineteen-eighties a heavy metal singer named Ronnie James Dio made it famous at concerts, and that's when everyone started calling it the devil horns. Today it's also used as a gang sign.

So whichever meaning you take, superstition about the evil eye, the devil, or a gang, there's nothing good in it for a Muslim. Avoid it.

Third, the I love you sign.

This one looks almost the same, but there's one difference. The thumb is out. Index, pinky and thumb, all up. That's American Sign Language, and it combines the letters I, L and Y, for I love you. It has nothing to do with the horns. Just make sure your thumb is actually out, because that's the only thing that separates it from the devil horns.

Fourth, the OK sign.

For almost everyone, this just means OK. But in twenty seventeen, people online started a hoax claiming it secretly meant white power. Some extremists then started using it for real, including the man who attacked two mosques in Christchurch, who flashed it in court. So in normal use, it's fine. But be careful with how and where you use it, and stay away from the upside down version people use as a joke.

So why does any of this matter?

The Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said that whoever imitates a people is one of them. Scholars explain that this is about things that are special to other people's religions, beliefs and identities, not everything non-Muslims happen to do. That's why some of these poses are a problem, and some aren't.

And actions are judged by intentions. Most people doing these poses have no idea what they mean, so this isn't about judging anyone in old photos. It's about knowing what your hands are saying, now that you do.

So next time someone pulls out a camera, skip the horns and skip the peace sign. You don't need any of them to look good in a picture.
