# Research: "3 Biggest Miracles in the Quran, Explained"

## What already exists
- Big channels post long lists ("20 shocking miracles", "43 Quran secrets", "10 mathematical miracles") built mostly on **scientific and number claims**: Big Bang, embryology, iron, "Code 19". Many are contested or stretched.
- Serious treatments exist (Yaqeen Institute's *Qur'anic Miracles* series), but they're long lectures and animations, not short teaching explainers.
- **The gap:** a short, calm, sourced video that picks only the three miracles scholars have always put first, and explains each properly. Choosing three and saying *why those three* is the hook.

## The three picks (strongest, best-sourced, least contested)

### 1. The challenge nobody could meet (the Quran's language)
- The Quran challenges anyone to produce something like it, and it **lowers the bar** over time: the whole Quran (17:88, 52:33–34) → ten surahs (11:13) → one surah (10:38, 2:23–24). 2:24 adds "and you will never be able to".
- It was revealed to a people whose whole culture was **poetry and eloquence**, and the Prophet ﷺ couldn't read or write (7:157, 29:48).
- His enemies tried everything else: they called him a poet, a madman, a sorcerer; they boycotted, fought and plotted to kill him. Producing one surah would have ended Islam instantly, and they never did.
- This is the miracle the Prophet ﷺ himself pointed to: every prophet was given signs, and what he was given is revelation (Sahih al-Bukhari 4981, Sahih Muslim 152).
- *Careful:* the story of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira's reaction is popular (tafsir of 74:18–25), but check the chain before quoting it as authentic.

### 2. Preserved word for word
- Allah promises to protect it: "Indeed, We sent down the Reminder, and indeed We will guard it" (15:9).
- It's memorised in full by millions today, and has been taught recitation-by-recitation in an unbroken chain.
- **The Birmingham manuscript:** two leaves (parts of Surahs 18–20, Hijazi script). In 2015 the University of Birmingham announced that Oxford had radiocarbon dated the parchment to **AD 568–645 (95.4% confidence)**. That puts it within or very close to the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime (c. 570–632), and the text matches today's Quran.
- *Careful:* radiocarbon dates the parchment, not the writing. Say "the parchment is dated to…". Don't bring in the Sana'a palimpsest without a proper explanation; it raises questions about early text variants that need much more time than this video has.

### 3. Prophecies that came true
- **The Romans (30:2–4):** "The Romans have been defeated in the nearest land, and after their defeat they will be victorious within a few years (*bid' sinin*)." Persia had crushed Byzantium (Jerusalem fell in 614). Then Heraclius turned it around, finally breaking Persia at **Nineveh (12 Dec 627)**. *Bid'* means three to nine; the Quraysh even bet Abu Bakr on it (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3191–3194; the gradings of these reports differ, so cite carefully).
- **Abu Lahab (Surah al-Masad, 111):** the surah says he would end in the Fire, which means he would die a disbeliever. He lived roughly **10 more years**, and all he had to do to "disprove" the Quran was say the shahada, even falsely. He never did, and died days after Badr (624).

## What to avoid
- "Code 19" / number miracles, and stretched science claims (Big Bang, embryology as proof). They're contested, and critics take them apart easily. Mention in one line: "the real miracles don't need stretching."
- Saying the Romans' victory came exactly at Badr as a fact (the reports differ). Keep it to "within a few years".
