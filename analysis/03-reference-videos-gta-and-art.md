## 2. Why the art video went viral

**Topic:** a question millions of Muslims (artists, animators, parents, people making faceless stickman content) genuinely have, with real stakes ("severest punishment").

**Title promise:** not "art is haram" but *"How to make it 100% HALAL, explained in 7 minutes"*. It promises a **solution** and a **time cost**.

**Hook (0:00–0:38):**
1. "Imagine you just spent hours perfecting a piece of art…" puts the viewer inside a scene in the second person.
2. A shock: the sahih hadith about picture makers. "That isn't even a da'if narration, that's graded as sahih."
3. Relief and authority: "But before you panic, you need to understand the actual science of Islamic jurisprudence."
4. A roadmap promise: "dropping the emotional internet debates… exact rules, authentic exceptions, how to keep your creativity 100% halal. Let's get into it."

**Structure:** the problem, then *why* (theology: idolatry and imitating creation), then the modern twist (pixels, iPads, AI), then a **beautiful Sunnah story** (Aisha's winged toy horse, the Prophet ﷺ smiling so widely his molars showed), then the legal principle (qiyas), then a **4-point checklist** (faceless, stylisation, nature, purpose), then the extremes on both sides, then a short close.
- It's **balanced**: a strict warning *and* real permission. It "dismantles the everything-is-haram argument" without being lax.
- It's **actionable**: viewers leave with a checklist they can apply to their own work. That kind of video gets saved and shared.
- **Authority markers** run through it: hadith numbers (Sunan Abi Dawud 4932), grades, the four madhhabs named, Ibn Abbas quoted, "a massive body of modern scholars".

## 3. The art video's visual language

| Element | How it's used |
|---|---|

| **Calligraphy seals for people** | Round gold or brown seals with the name in Arabic calligraphy for the Prophet ﷺ, Aisha (RA) and Ibn Abbas (RA). People are named and visually present without being depicted. |

## 4. What works in our GTA edit (keep)

- **Real images of the actual thing:** the real GTA case in a hand (pixelated), real queues at launch, a real "Full Alien Side Quest" screenshot. Viewers instantly know what we're talking about.
- **Emotional stock photos:** hellfire, a silhouette in danger, a person asleep at a laptop, a bloodshot eye, a dark grinning face for "dunya".
- **Memes for relief:** "Cool, I don't care", Drake.
- **Number cards and "Reason:" labels** for clear structure.
- **Checklist with green ticks** ("Instead you could have: spent time with family / learning your deen / being productive").
- **Contrast cards:** "today: −1 / tomorrow: +1,000,000", "2 hours a day → 14 hours a week → 100s of hours a year".
- **Hadi scenes** for the narrator moments (holding the game, throwing it in the bin, Share button, Assalamu Alaikum).

## 5. What to integrate into our style (the upgrade)

1. **Real images first.** Every topic noun gets a real image: the actual post, poster, screenshot, product or place, blurred where needed. Illustrations and diagrams are for *ideas*, not for things that exist.
2. **Calligraphy seals** whenever we quote the Prophet ﷺ, a Sahabi or a scholar.
3. **Book cover + reference + grade** on every hadith (the art-video card layout).
4. **Word-by-word key sentences**, with the next word fading in from grey and key words in red or green, for the 3–5 most important lines in each video.
5. **Arrows that draw on to short labels** to explain *why* (image → 2–3 reasons).
6. **Give Hadi more acting:** add poses for the whiteboard/explain, a confused "?" and a raised-finger "but…". Use him at every turn in the argument, not just the start and end.
7. **Two speeds:** hook and lists at ~1 s per visual; explanations and evidence at ~2 s with more stillness, so viewers can read.
8. **Structure:** hook with "Imagine you…" or "Many of you have seen…", then the shock, then "before you panic", then a roadmap, then the points, then a **practical checklist**, then a short close. Titles that promise a solution where possible ("…and what to do instead").
9. **A red lower-third chapter title** at each section start.
