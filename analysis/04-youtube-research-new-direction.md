## What the winners share (and our videos don't)

1. **A journey, not a list of rules.** The viewer *goes through* something: stage 1 → stage 9, level 1 → level 7, first night in the grave. Each stage is a mini-reveal, so you keep watching to see the next one. Our videos are "point 1, point 2, point 3" arguments, which feel like a lecture, so people leave once they've guessed the conclusion.
2. **"You" is the main character.** "Your first night in the grave", "what happens when *you* pray tahajjud". Our videos talk *about* other people's posts and games.
3. **The unseen world is the biggest topic.** Barzakh, the grave, the Sirat, Jannah levels, signs of the Day of Judgement. These are curiosity topics nobody can "already know the answer" to, so there's no reason to click away.
4. **Built-in progression = built-in retention.** "Every stage explained in 9 minutes" tells the viewer exactly what they'll get and makes them wait for the last (most intense) stage.
5. **Story > explanation.** Quran stories told like anime episodes get 100–500K within days.
6. **Emotion is the payoff:** fear (the grave), hope (Jannah), wonder (stories), or laughter (relatable humour). Our videos mostly deliver *information*.

## Recommended new direction

**"The Journey" format:** Hadi (and the viewer, in the second person) travels through a sequence of stages from the Quran and authentic hadith. One stage = one scene, with a stage counter on screen, and the most intense stage held back for last.

Structure of each video:
1. Cold open on the most intense moment (5 s), then "Let's start from the beginning."
2. Stage counter on screen the whole time (Stage 1/7 …).
3. Each stage: what happens, the evidence (ayah/hadith card), and one "what this means for you" line.
4. Final stage: the biggest emotional moment.
5. Short close: what to do today so your journey goes well.

Keep the "calling out trends" videos as an occasional second format, because they built the channel.
