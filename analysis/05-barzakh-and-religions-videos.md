# Reference analysis: "Every Stage of Barzakh Explained in 9 minutes" (3M views) + "What Happens After Death in Every Religion Explained"

Method: full word-timed transcripts, contact sheets every 5 s across both videos, 1-fps strips of a whiteboard build and an image-overlay sequence, and measurements of the visual-change rate and the share of white-background frames.

| | Barzakh (9:04) | Every Religion (14:19) |
|---|---|---|
| Big visual change every (median) | 3.1 s | 7.3 s (small additions in between) |
| White whiteboard frames | 29% | **94%** |
| Illustrated scene frames | ~65% | ~5% |
| Frames with no motion | 81% | 57% |

## 1. How they script

### Barzakh (story-led)
1. **Hook = a question in the second person + an image of it happening:** "What happens the exact moment after you're buried? Your family walks away. The soil covers you. Everyone thinks you're gone." **Very short sentences, one image each.**
2. **Myth-bust:** "Most Muslims think death means… Wrong. Absolutely wrong."
3. **Authority:** "This comes from authentic hadith in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim."
4. **Promise/roadmap:** "In the next few minutes I'm walking you through all seven stages. By the end, you'll know exactly what's waiting for you, and how to prepare right now."
5. **A named example character:** "Let me show you with an example. This is Ahmad. He died three days ago." The whole video follows Ahmad.
6. **Each stage:** title card, then present-tense narration with sensory detail ("he hears footsteps fading above the soil"), then **two branches** ("If Ahmad lived righteously… But if he wasted his life…"), then the evidence on a chalkboard (QURAN / HADITH), then a **cliffhanger into the next stage** ("Two beings are approaching his grave.").
7. **Ending:** reflection questions ("Which Ahmad will you be?"), then hope ("The beautiful truth is you're still breathing"), then a practical list ("5 specific traps"… including **"I'll pray later"**).

### Every Religion (explainer)
- Calm, factual, **one concrete detail per sentence** ("washed three times", "within 24 hours", "12 months").
- **Pattern interrupts every 30–40 s:** "But here's what most people don't know." / "But here's where it gets intense." / "And here's something you will not believe."
- **Every spoken noun becomes an icon on the board.**

## 2. How they narrate images (Barzakh)

- **One recurring master scene** (the grave cross-section), reused for minutes and changed with **overlays**, not new pictures:
  - **thought / speech bubbles** that pop in with the dialogue ("Do you know who I am?", "I am your good deeds")
  - a **window** inserted into the grave wall: a garden or fire
  - **light**: a glow filling the grave, or the grave going dark
  - **objects**: a ribcage drawn on top when the grave squeezes; angel wings appear
  - **big text** on the scene ("800 YEARS")
- **Caption line at the top** in italic handwritten caps that types on ("IF AHMED LIVED RIGHTEOUSLY").
- Small **emoji / sticker reactions** pop on the scene (thinking face, lips for "his tongue betrays him").
- **Black screen + white text** for heavy moments ("HIS GRAVE EXPANDS", "HE CAN BREATHE").
- **Chalkboard card** labelled "QURAN" or "HADITH" with the stickman pointing at it, for evidence.
- **Moves:** slow push-ins on the scene, cuts between wide and close, and mostly stillness while overlays pop. The character is **not** in every frame.

## 3. How the whiteboard works (Every Religion, the main thing to copy)

1. **One board holds a whole thought: 3–8 narration lines, 15–60 s.** Items are added *one per sentence* and **never removed** until the thought is finished; then a **hard cut to a clean white board**.
2. **Reading path:** items are laid out left to right, top to bottom, linked by **hand-drawn black curved arrows** that draw on as each new item appears.
3. **Item types (mixed freely):**
   - small clip-art / 3D stickers (graves, people, scales, books, angels, a clock with "24h")
   - **small real photos** in rounded rectangles (the Kaaba, a funeral, a crowd)
   - **numbers in red** ("x3", "12 Months")
   - **red text labels** for names and terms ("kafan", "Barzakh", "Mizan", "Gehenna")
   - **yellow highlight boxes** for transition phrases ("After that", "Real journey starts after burial", "Day of Judgment", "If possible")
   - **pills**: red for negatives ("isn't permanent", "not eternal"), green for positives ("It's pure", "It is temporary")
   - **stamps**: RESPECT, REJECTED, APPROVED
4. **The narrator stickman** only appears on some boards, at the side, reacting (thinking, pointing, smiling) for the "here's what most people don't know" moments.
5. **Motion:** items pop in (quick scale), arrows draw on, and the camera mostly stays still. Occasionally there's a slow zoom out as the board fills.

## 4. What we take for hadi.teachings

- **Script:** our short hook, then myth-bust, authority, roadmap, a **named example character (Hadi as "this is Hadi")**, sections with two branches and a cliffhanger, evidence on a chalkboard, pattern-interrupt lines, then reflection, hope and a practical list.
- **Scenes:** one or two **master scenes** (the classroom, the bedroom) reused with overlays: bubbles, clocks, light, windows, captions.
- **Whiteboard:** **accumulating boards** (3–8 lines each), with icons, arrows, red labels, yellow highlight boxes, pills and stamps, and a clean cut only when the thought ends.
- **Hadi is not in every frame.** He's the story character in scenes and an occasional reactor on boards.
- **Evidence:** a chalkboard card labelled QURAN / HADITH (English + reference), with Hadi pointing.
