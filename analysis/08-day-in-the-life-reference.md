# Reference: "What ancient Muslim women actually did all day, hour by hour" (6:24)

## Visual rhythm
- About 96 different pictures in 384 s: a new picture every ~4 s, joined with soft crossfades (almost no hard cuts).
- ~60% full illustrated scenes (warm cartoon backgrounds, small characters), slow zoom-in on each.
- ~40% white-background "whiteboard" frames: a stick figure plus **one or two objects**, sometimes a big red ✕ or green ✓, sometimes a tiny handwritten caption at the top ("HOUR BY HOUR", "6:00 AM — DAWN", "ANCIENT TAKEOUT?", "MODERN TRAP?"). No paragraphs of text, no labels everywhere.
- Section markers are just the time written small on the first picture of each hour.

## Script structure
1. **Modern pain, second person (0:00–0:32):** "Right now, your morning probably starts…" Concrete, relatable details.
2. **Rewind (0:32–0:48):** "Now rewind a thousand years. No washing machine, no gas stove…" Each missing thing gets its own crossed-out picture.
3. **The assumption (0:48–0:58):** "By simple math, her day should have been torture… right?"
4. **Myth-bust + authority (0:58–1:27):** "Except when you open real historical accounts…" Then a promise of three surprising things.
5. **Hour by hour (1:27–5:47):** dawn → morning → 10 AM → noon → 4 PM → sunset → night, each with sensory detail and one "wild detail most people don't know".
6. **Contrast ending (5:47–6:24):** "Modern life gave you… yet…" Then the one-line lesson.

## What we change for hadi.teachings
- **Men only** (channel rule), using the two thumbnail characters: stick-figure men in a white and a brown thobe with headbands.
- The Prophet ﷺ and the Companions are never drawn as characters; they're only referred to. Generic townspeople are fine.
- Madinah in the Prophet's ﷺ time (7th century), built on **authentic hadith** rather than vague "historical accounts". References on screen only.
- More interesting than the reference: one strong "most people don't know" detail per hour, and clear modern contrasts.
